TITLE: VA Rating for Sleep Apnea: 0, 30, 50 and 100 Percent Explained
SUMMARY: Sleep apnea is rated under diagnostic code 6847. A prescribed CPAP currently supports 50%. Here are the levels and what evidence matters.

<p>VA rates sleep apnea under diagnostic code 6847 (38 CFR 4.97). Right now the most common rating is <strong>50%</strong>, which applies when you need a breathing assistance device such as a CPAP machine.</p>
<p>VA proposed new sleep apnea criteria in 2022 that would change these levels. As of October 2026 that proposal is not final, so the levels below still apply. Check VA's official sources before you file in case that changes.</p>

<h2>Current rating levels</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>100%:</strong> chronic respiratory failure with carbon dioxide retention or cor pulmonale, or requires a tracheostomy.</li>
  <li><strong>50%:</strong> requires use of a breathing assistance device such as a CPAP machine.</li>
  <li><strong>30%:</strong> persistent daytime hypersomnolence (ongoing daytime sleepiness).</li>
  <li><strong>0%:</strong> no symptoms, but documented sleep-disordered breathing.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What a claim needs</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>A diagnosis from a sleep study.</strong> VA generally requires a sleep study to confirm sleep apnea.</li>
  <li><strong>A link to service.</strong> Either symptoms that began in service (snoring, gasping, daytime sleepiness noted by others or in records) or a medical opinion that another service-connected condition caused or worsened it.</li>
  <li><strong>The current severity.</strong> A CPAP prescription, or records of daytime sleepiness.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Secondary service connection</h2>
<p>Many veterans connect sleep apnea to another service-connected condition, such as weight gain from limited mobility, PTSD, or medications. That route needs a medical opinion explaining how the service-connected condition caused or worsened the sleep apnea. A general article or statistic is not enough on its own.</p>

<h2>Evidence that helps</h2>
<ul>
  <li>The sleep study report and the CPAP prescription.</li>
  <li>Statements from a spouse, roommate or fellow service member who saw you snore, stop breathing or fall asleep during the day, in service or soon after.</li>
  <li>Service records mentioning fatigue, snoring or sleep problems.</li>
  <li>A medical opinion if you are claiming it as secondary to another condition.</li>
</ul>
