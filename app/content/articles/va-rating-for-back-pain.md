TITLE: VA Rating for Back Pain: How Range of Motion Sets the Percentage
SUMMARY: Back conditions are usually rated by how far you can bend forward, measured in degrees. Here are the levels and how to prepare for the exam.

<p>Most back conditions, such as a lumbosacral strain (diagnostic code 5237) or degenerative arthritis of the spine (5242), are rated with VA's General Rating Formula for Diseases and Injuries of the Spine (38 CFR 4.71a). For the lower and middle back, the rating mostly depends on how far you can bend forward, measured in degrees during the exam.</p>

<h2>Lower and middle back (thoracolumbar) levels</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>10%:</strong> forward flexion greater than 60° but not greater than 85°; or combined range of motion greater than 120° but not greater than 235°; or muscle spasm, guarding or localized tenderness that does not cause an abnormal walk or spine shape; or a vertebral body fracture with loss of 50% or more of its height.</li>
  <li><strong>20%:</strong> forward flexion greater than 30° but not greater than 60°; or combined range of motion not greater than 120°; or muscle spasm or guarding severe enough to cause an abnormal walk or spine shape (such as scoliosis).</li>
  <li><strong>40%:</strong> forward flexion of 30° or less, or favorable ankylosis (fusion) of the entire thoracolumbar spine.</li>
  <li><strong>50%:</strong> unfavorable ankylosis of the entire thoracolumbar spine.</li>
  <li><strong>100%:</strong> unfavorable ankylosis of the entire spine.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Nerve pain is rated separately</h2>
<p>If back problems cause pain, numbness or tingling down a leg (radiculopathy or sciatica), VA rates each affected leg separately under the nerve codes, in addition to the back rating. Make sure the examiner knows about leg symptoms.</p>

<h2>Flare-ups and pain count</h2>
<p>The examiner should note where pain begins during movement, and should estimate how much more motion you lose during flare-ups or after repeated use. Be ready to describe your flare-ups: how often, how long, and what you can't do during one.</p>

<h2>Prepare for the exam</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Don't push through pain to bend farther. Stop where pain stops you, as you would at home.</li>
  <li>Bring a short list of flare-up frequency, missed work and daily tasks you avoid.</li>
  <li>Mention any leg symptoms, bladder or bowel problems, and any time a doctor prescribed bed rest.</li>
</ul>
