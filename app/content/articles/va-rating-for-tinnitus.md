TITLE: VA Rating for Tinnitus: What the 10% Rating Covers
SUMMARY: Tinnitus is rated at 10% under diagnostic code 6260, one rating whether it's in one ear or both. Here's what VA needs to see.

<p>The VA rates recurrent tinnitus at <strong>10%</strong> under diagnostic code 6260 (38 CFR 4.87). That is the only rating level for tinnitus on its own, and VA assigns a single 10% whether you hear it in one ear, both ears, or in your head.</p>
<p>A proposal to change how tinnitus is rated was published in 2022. As of October 2026 it has not become final, so the 10% rating described here still applies. Check VA's official sources before you file in case that changes.</p>

<h2>Quick answer</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Rating: 10%, diagnostic code 6260.</li>
  <li>One rating only, no matter how many ears are affected.</li>
  <li>Tinnitus can be rated separately from hearing loss.</li>
  <li>It is not rated separately when it is a symptom used to rate another ear condition, such as Meniere's disease.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What VA looks for</h2>
<p>Tinnitus is something only you can hear, so your own description matters. VA generally accepts that a veteran is able to report ringing, buzzing or hissing. The claim usually turns on two questions: is it recurrent, and is it connected to service?</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Recurrent:</strong> it comes back regularly, not once after a single loud event.</li>
  <li><strong>Service connection:</strong> noise exposure in service, such as weapons fire, aircraft, engines, generators or blasts. Your job, unit and deployment records help show it.</li>
  <li><strong>When it started:</strong> a clear statement of when you first noticed it and how often it happens now.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Evidence that helps</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Your DD-214 and personnel records showing your job (many jobs have a high probability of noise exposure).</li>
  <li>Hearing test results from service and after service, if you have them.</li>
  <li>A personal statement (VA Form 21-4138) describing the sound, when it started and how it affects sleep, focus or work.</li>
  <li>Buddy statements from people who served with you, if your records don't show the noise exposure.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Telling the examiner it "comes and goes" without saying how often. Say how many days a week, and for how long.</li>
  <li>Forgetting to mention when it started. A clear start date during or soon after service matters.</li>
  <li>Expecting more than 10% for tinnitus alone. Higher combined ratings come from other conditions, such as hearing loss or conditions secondary to tinnitus.</li>
</ul>
