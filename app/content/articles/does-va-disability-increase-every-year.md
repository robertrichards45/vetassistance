<h2>Detailed Explanation</h2>
<p>In general, annual VA disability increases depends on your evidence, the VA's rating criteria, and the effective dates tied to your claim. The VA looks at medical records, service records, and any supporting statements to determine eligibility and amounts.</p>
<p>When the VA reviews a claim, it applies the CFR criteria for the condition or issue at hand. This is why the exact language in your medical records and exam notes matters. The VA compares your evidence to the criteria and assigns the level that best fits the record.</p>
<p>If your question involves payments, dependency, or timelines, the VA relies on rate tables and internal processing steps. These can change year to year and can shift due to federal holidays or end-of-month timing.</p>
<p>For C&P exams, the examiner documents symptoms, history, and functional impact. Those findings are combined with the rest of your evidence. The exam does not replace your medical records; it adds to them.</p>
<p>If your situation includes an appeal or supplemental claim, timelines and outcomes may differ. Each review lane has its own evidence rules, so be sure you know what can and cannot be added.</p>
<p>Finally, decision letters explain why the VA decided the way it did. Reading the reasons and bases section is key to knowing what to fix or add.</p>

<h2>Common Mistakes Veterans Make</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Assuming a payment amount without checking updated rate tables.</li>
  <li>Leaving out key evidence such as a diagnosis, service event, or nexus.</li>
  <li>Missing deadlines or not responding to VA requests on time.</li>
  <li>Using vague language instead of describing frequency and impact.</li>
  <li>Not reviewing decision letters to understand the VA's reasoning.</li>
  <li>Submitting duplicate or conflicting records without clarification.</li>
  <li>Relying on old information that no longer reflects current rules.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How This Affects Your VA Claim</h2>
<p>Your answer to this question can affect your timeline, your evidence strategy, and the final rating or payment. For example, effective dates can change how far back pay goes, and exam results can change the rating level.</p>
<p>In many cases, small documentation gaps can lead to delays or lower ratings. Clear evidence tied directly to the criteria reduces confusion and helps decision-makers apply the correct standards.</p>
<p>If you have a prior denial or an appeal, use the decision letter to identify the exact issue the VA cited. That helps you target the missing evidence rather than resubmitting the same records.</p>
<p>When dependency or tax questions are involved, confirm eligibility and rules with official sources. That prevents incorrect expectations and helps you plan accurately.</p>
