TITLE: VA Rating for PTSD: How the 0 to 100 Percent Levels Work
SUMMARY: PTSD is rated under diagnostic code 9411 using VA's general formula for mental disorders. What matters most is how symptoms affect work and relationships.

<p>VA rates PTSD under diagnostic code 9411 using the General Rating Formula for Mental Disorders (38 CFR 4.130). The levels are 0, 10, 30, 50, 70 and 100 percent. The rating depends on how much your symptoms affect your ability to work and maintain relationships, not on the diagnosis alone.</p>
<p>VA proposed a new mental health rating formula in 2022. As of October 2026 it has not become final, so the formula below still applies. Check VA's official sources before you file in case that changes.</p>

<h2>The levels in plain language</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>100%:</strong> total occupational and social impairment.</li>
  <li><strong>70%:</strong> deficiencies in most areas, such as work, school, family relations, judgment, thinking or mood.</li>
  <li><strong>50%:</strong> reduced reliability and productivity at work and in relationships.</li>
  <li><strong>30%:</strong> occasional decrease in work efficiency, with intermittent periods of being unable to perform tasks, though generally functioning satisfactorily.</li>
  <li><strong>10%:</strong> mild or transient symptoms that reduce efficiency only during periods of significant stress, or that are controlled by medication.</li>
  <li><strong>0%:</strong> a diagnosed condition, but symptoms are not severe enough to interfere with work or social functioning or to require continuous medication.</li>
</ul>
<p>The regulation lists example symptoms for each level. Those are examples, not a checklist: VA looks at the overall effect of your symptoms, how often they happen and how severe they are.</p>

<h2>What a claim needs</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>A current PTSD diagnosis.</strong></li>
  <li><strong>An in-service stressor.</strong> Combat, fear of hostile military or terrorist activity, and military sexual trauma each have their own evidence rules that can make the stressor easier to establish.</li>
  <li><strong>A medical link</strong> between the stressor and your symptoms, usually from the VA examination.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Describe your worst days, honestly</h2>
<ul>
  <li>How often symptoms happen: nightmares per week, panic attacks per month, days of missed work.</li>
  <li>What you've lost or avoided: jobs, relationships, crowds, driving, family events.</li>
  <li>Statements from a spouse, friend or co-worker who sees the effects. They often describe things you might leave out.</li>
</ul>

<h2>If you are struggling right now</h2>
<p>Call the Veterans Crisis Line: dial 988 and press 1, or text 838255. It is free, confidential and available 24/7.</p>
